# 12-h tier readout (2026-06-12)

Four Kaggle CPU kernels (mg-long12h-s51..s54), seeds 51-54, launched the night
of June 11 with the campaign config (mixed boot modular-fraction 0.5, refresh
1500, harshness 1.6, structural rate 0.30, max-blocks 3 per operator step,
grace 150/0.35). Collected June 12 ~09:20. Pre-registered questions and the
report tool: `analysis/retention_report.py` (commit 3d6bf78).

## Q1 — held vs delayed erosion (anchor: blocks_mean at t≈11k vs end)

| seed | t11k blocks | end blocks | end tick | verdict at anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s51 | 1.88 | 1.77 | 34 800 | HELD |
| s52 | 3.28 | 2.90 | 15 700 | HELD |
| s53 | 1.65 | 1.50 | 11 900 | HELD |
| s54 | 2.49 | **7.56** | 21 000 | HELD (grew 3×) |

All four pass the pre-registered ≥85% criterion. But the anchor undersells the
trajectories (end ticks differ because wall-clock budget ÷ population size):

- **s51** (tiny ecology, 9-311-22 neural): blocks 2.86 → 1.77 over 35k ticks.
  Slow erosion in a near-empty world; held only relative to an already-eroded
  t11k value.
- **s52** (large ecology, ~500-700 neural, **5 dynasties** — first true
  multi-dynasty world): peaked 4.11 at t7.9k, eroding by end (2.90, capacity
  33 → 18). Competition did not protect structure here.
- **s53** (largest pop): peaked 3.75 at t6k, crashed to 1.50 by end. The t11k
  anchor sits *after* the crash, so "HELD" is technically true and
  substantively misleading. Erosion, not retention.
- **s54 — the event.** Inverse arc: low early (1.90 at t5.3k), then sustained
  late accumulation 2.22 → 5.32 → **7.56 blocks, capacity 46.3**, under a
  *single* dynasty, 189 neural at end, still rising at the wall. First world
  where structure compounds late at sustained h1.6 instead of eroding.

## Q2 — dynasty competition vs retention

6-h tier suggested multi-dynasty ↔ retention (s45). The 12-h tier **breaks
that correlation**: s52 (5 dynasties) eroded; s54 (1 dynasty) exploded. With
n=5 worlds across tiers the honest state is: no stable relationship between
dynasty count and structural retention. The s45 observation was likely
world-luck.

## Standing

- Retention at the pre-registered anchor: 4/4 HELD — the 6-h "erosion is the
  default" conclusion does NOT extrapolate to a one-way slide at 12 h.
- The interesting object is now **s54's late runaway**: what changed at
  ~t12-15k? Post-mortem targets: structure_events genealogy around the
  takeoff, whether refresh cycles align with accumulation steps, and what the
  7.5-block individuals' capacity is spent on (census + run_digest
  biographies).
- Anchor lesson for the next pre-registration: compare end vs *peak*, not end
  vs a fixed tick — fixed-tick anchors can land after a crash (s53) and
  certify erosion as retention.
