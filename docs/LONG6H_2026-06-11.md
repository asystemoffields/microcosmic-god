## Post-mortems

**What killed 342: predation against a thin pool.** Epoch death analysis
(aggregate diffs): t4500–6700 saw 174 neural predation deaths + 50 starvation
against a standing pool of ~25–50 — the dynasty bred capacity-heavy children
into predators' mouths faster than it could replace them. Starvation's share
rose as the pool thinned. Its notable lives are all morality plays: #441
(958 tool successes, 0 offspring) starved; #459 (3 blocks, cap 39) starved at
age 548; the two 15-offspring breeders were both predated.

**341's structural retreat was selective death, not adaptive pruning.** Prune
share of structural ops stayed flat (~0.18–0.22) across the entire erosion;
step *volume* fell with the modular pool. The mean fell because big
individuals died or failed to breed, while the mutation mix stayed
growth-biased throughout.

**Single-dynasty monopoly confirmed at 6-h scale.** 341: lineage 445 took
1,956 of 1,983 steps. 342: lineage 436 took 198 of 202. No world produced a
second structural dynasty that mattered (e6-seed335's lineage 448 remains the
only sighting, and it was a minority). Refresh cycles did NOT spawn
competing structural lineages; they ground the incumbent down.

## Campaign finding (locals; n=3 + e6's n=2 at 45 min)

Structure **explodes when ecology is permissive** (post-bottleneck booms with
empty niches) and **erodes under sustained pressure**. At h1.6, capacity does
not pay its way over hours: predation and upkeep select against blocks once
the boom ends. Both dynasties peaked within ~2,500 ticks of their boom and
declined or died after. The substrate can now *discover* structure (the
evolvability program worked: blk_max 8, cap_max 91 observed) — but nothing in
the world *demands* it, so it is selected away at equilibrium.

**Implication:** the next lever is not more selection pressure (h1.6 already
grinds structure down) but a world feature only capacity can exploit — i.e.
exactly the ranked feature list (cue reliability, patch recovery recalibrated,
within-lifetime contract drift). Run the same campaign with one
capacity-demanding feature enabled and compare erosion slopes.
